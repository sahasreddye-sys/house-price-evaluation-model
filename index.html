      How close it gets

      Every estimate here is out-of-fold: the model that priced a home never saw it during training. The benchmark is the county’s own assessed value.

      Where to be careful

        Against real sales. County values are not sale prices. Against recent Alpharetta sales (warranty deed, land and building, $100k+), both the county and the model come in under the price paid. Ratio is value ÷ sale price; COD is the average spread around that ratio.

        Changes in v2

        v1 priced Alpharetta from four inputs and covered a 1,653-home sample of Cumming. v2 reads the buildings, covers all of Forsyth County, and tests its range.

        Building details for Alpharetta
        Square footage across every structure on the lot, year built, beds, baths, stories, basement, exterior, fireplaces, topography and road frontage.
        All of Forsyth County
        80,306 homes instead of a 1,653-home sample from Cumming.
        No county land value
        v1 leaned on the assessor’s land value, which nobody outside the assessor’s office would have. v2 drops it and still lands within 0.1 points of v1’s median error.
        Coordinates
        Latitude and longitude, so the model can separate streets that share a neighborhood code.
        A tested range
        The 80% range is set from the model’s own errors, then checked on a held-out half of homes: 79.8% coverage in Alpharetta, 80.0% in Forsyth.
        A harder test
        Holding out whole neighborhoods, to see how the model does somewhere it has never been.

        Data: Fulton County and Forsyth County tax assessor parcel records, tax year 2025. Two LightGBM models, 600 trees each, trained on log county value with 5-fold cross-validation. Estimates follow assessed value, not market price, and are not an appraisal.
